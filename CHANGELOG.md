# Changelog

All notable changes to this module are listed here. Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

The first version of Call Feedback: a post-call keypad survey for MikoPBX 2026.6.34 or later.

Added:

- **Surveys set up on the module page** (Modules → Call Feedback, administrators only).
  - Versions in a family (draft, active, archived). An active version's questions and prompts are fixed so results stay comparable; changing the wording means a new version. Rules and routes can still change on a live version.
  - 1–3 questions on fixed scales: CSAT 1–5, NPS 0–10 ("1 then 0" for 10), CES 1–5 (asked as "how easy was it", so higher is better everywhere) and yes/no. Question 1 is the headline score. Prompts are chosen from the PBX's sound files; timeouts, retries and an invalid-key prompt per survey. Prompt language English or Russian.
  - Rules: minimum talk time, days and hours, the share of calls to survey, a frequency cap per number, an exclusion list and a limit on surveys running at once. A survey ends within 3 minutes whatever is pressed. Calls a rule skips are counted per day, survey version, route and reason.
  - Runs on chosen incoming and outgoing routes, one active survey per route. With no active survey the module adds no dialplan to any call.
  - A test call rings an extension and plays the version; one is needed before activation. Activation also checks that every prompt plays, the routes are free, the PBX's default incoming action doesn't play a sound file (which would remove the survey hook from every route), and no other post-call survey module is enabled.
- **How calls reach the survey.** Inbound, the caller's channel continues after the agent hangs up and the survey runs from the route's after-dial hook (with `Gosub`, so custom after-dial dialplan still runs). Outbound, `F(...)` moves the customer into the survey when the agent hangs up. Only calls an agent answered are surveyed, with talk time measured from the agent's answer, so calls that only reached an IVR, voicemail or a hangup action are skipped and time on hold doesn't count. Blind-transferred calls and callers who hang up first are not surveyed.
- **Results stored safely.** A hangup handler runs one AGI script after the customer has gone; it validates every value against a fixed pattern and queues the record for the module's worker, which validates it again, keeps only answers on the question's scale, and stores the session, so partial answers count. Once the call is in the CDR, the worker fills in the agent, queue, wait and talk time from its legs; sessions under the minimum talk time are kept but left out of scores, and sessions whose call never appears in the CDR are discarded.
- **Results pages.** Overview: four cards (the headline score with its responses, average and change against the previous period; response rate; resolution rate, or completion rate when the survey has no yes/no question; open follow-ups, which opens the list), the score over time and the distribution of answers. The trend is daily on short ranges with enough responses and weekly otherwise, so it isn't mostly gaps; days or weeks with too few responses show as hollow grey points. Breakdowns by agent, queue, line, direction, hour, weekday, time to answer and talk time, with a bar for each group's score. Survey health, per version: how many eligible calls (answered by an agent on the survey's routes) were surveyed and how many surveyed callers reached the end; where callers left, as one bar and per question, with the question most callers leave at in red; and the calls each rule left unsurveyed, with calls found too short in the CDR after the survey counted under "too short". Scores are per version and appear only with enough responses (default 20). Every chart has a PNG download and every results table a CSV download. Charts use a tree-shaken Apache ECharts 6.1.0.
- **Follow-ups.** A headline score at or below the detractor threshold (CSAT ≤ 2, NPS ≤ 6, CES ≤ 2 by default) opens a case once the call is confirmed in the CDR, and emails the chosen recipients. The list is filtered by status with a count on each filter and on the tab; a dialog shows the case and holds the call-back and the status and note, with who resolved it.
- **Call-back from a follow-up**, with safeguards against toll fraud: it rings a chosen extension and then calls the customer through the outgoing routes; one click only for numbers starting with a prefix listed in Settings (none by default, never + or 00), any other number typed in to match; every call-back logged; the call-back itself is not surveyed.
- **Emails:** detractor alerts and an optional daily or weekly summary, as plain text in the PBX's language, through the PBX's mail settings. They never carry the caller's number.
- **Privacy:** callers' numbers follow the PBX's call-history retention and are then removed, keeping scores; frequency-cap keys never outlive it; "delete all data for a number"; uninstalling removes the AGI link and astdb keys the core leaves behind. No audio is recorded.
- **Security:** admin-only pages with the role checked in the module; writes need an AJAX POST with the admin's token; nothing typed by an admin or sent by a caller reaches the dialplan; no new AMI users, ports or outbound connections; survey contexts can't be dialled from a phone or trunk.
- **Built-in prompts and an example survey.** The module ships its own prompts in English and Russian (intro, CSAT, NPS, CES and "was your issue resolved?" questions, invalid key, thank-you) in `Sounds/en-en` and `Sounds/ru-ru`. The core installs them into the PBX's sound folders when the module is enabled, converts them, and removes them when it is disabled, so they never appear in the PBX's Sound files list. A survey plays them in its own language. They are stored as negative prompt IDs, which can't clash with the PBX's sound files, and the dialplan only ever plays names from the module's fixed list. The editor's prompt menus list them first, then the PBX's sound files. A new install starts with "Example Survey", a draft that uses them (CSAT, then "was your issue resolved?"), in Russian when the PBX's prompt language is Russian. It has no routes and no results, is created once per module database (never on an update, and never again once deleted), and only needs a route and a test call to go live. **New Survey** offers the same as templates: CSAT, NPS or CES, each followed by "was your issue resolved?" and using the built-in prompts in the chosen language, or a blank survey; the name is filled in from the template unless one has been typed. The recordings were made for the module and are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0 (`Sounds/LICENSE`); they are levelled together and stored as 8 kHz, 16-bit mono WAV.
- **Surveys tab**: one row per survey with its headline scale, language and current version, links to its other versions, whether a draft is ready to activate, and a Results button that opens the Overview on it; surveys with only archived versions are folded away. A draft can be deleted (with its test calls; its routes too when it was the survey's only version), so a survey made by mistake doesn't stay in the list. In the editor:
  - a version bar to move between a survey's versions, and Open Draft instead of New Version when a draft already exists;
  - unsaved changes are tracked: test calls and Activate wait for Save, since they use the saved version, and leaving the editor asks first;
  - routes another survey already runs on are shown as taken, with its name, instead of failing at activation;
  - on a draft of a live survey, a note that routes are shared by all versions and a change applies to the live version on save.
- **Settings page** in sections (Follow-Ups, Emails, Call-Backs, Results and Limits), saved with one Save button that stays off until something changes; detractor thresholds chosen as score ranges for each scale; a link to the PBX's mail settings when email notifications are off. Excluded numbers (with when each was added) and "delete all data for a number" apply at once and sit below, and the Privacy section shows how long the PBX keeps its call history, with a link to where that is set.
- **Confirmations in the page's own dialog** (deleting a draft, stopping a survey, deleting a number's data, placing a call-back, leaving the editor with unsaved changes) rather than the browser's `confirm()`, which some browsers and embedded views never show and answer "Cancel" at once, so the action silently did nothing.
- **Interface in English and Russian**, styled to match the Call Insights module: the same colours, spacing, section headings with their download buttons, summary cards and quiet tables, and Title Case for English headings, tabs, buttons, menu choices and field labels, with units in brackets, such as "Minimum Talk Time (Seconds)".
- **Documentation:** a README for people deciding whether to install the module and for admins setting it up, laid out like the Call Insights README: features, screenshots taken on a test PBX, requirements (including what stops a survey being activated), installing, updating and uninstalling, setting up a survey, how it works in plain terms, its limits, privacy and security. How a survey runs inside Asterisk moves to CONTRIBUTING.md. The screenshots are left out of release zips.
- **Development:** 252 unit tests that need no PBX; CallSim, a real-call test harness with 21 survey scenarios run against MikoPBX 2026.6.34 (skip counts are checked against the survey that skipped the call); PHPStan against the core release the module targets; a check for banned functions; reproducible packaging and a draft-release workflow.
